Crosswind Drift Model

Only the part of the wind blowing across the flight path pushes the ball sideways, so the drift depends on which way the throw goes. Most local football fields run roughly north–south, which is the default here.

The wind pushes the ball with a drag force, and dividing that by the ball's mass gives a sideways acceleration. Because the ball starts with no sideways motion at all, the drift builds up as ½ a t² — the same formula as a dropped object, except the "gravity" is the wind's shove and it is weaker for a heavy ball. A football drifts far more than a baseball: it is lighter for the area it presents, and it is in the air nearly three times as long.

Modelled estimate, not a measurement. Assumes a steady crosswind for the whole flight, no spin or Magnus effect, and a constant drag coefficient. Air density is taken from this station's own pressure and temperature — at basin elevation that is about 20% thinner than sea level, so about 20% less push than a sea-level calculator would give.

Prediction Game — coin-flip trainer (prototype)

A demonstration of how forecasts are scored. The outcome here is a fair coin, not the weather — swapping in a real basin forecast is the next step.

  • You start with 1000 tokens and split them between two outcomes.
  • The house quotes each side at 50%. A winning stake pays back stake ÷ quote, so a correct 50% call doubles that stake and the other stake is lost.
  • Those are fair odds: your bank neither grows nor shrinks on average, whatever you choose.
  • Each round scores you the log of (your probability ÷ the house's). Match the house and you score zero.
  • Against a coin you have no edge, so the long-run best play is an even split — and the bank barely moves. That is the lesson.
  • Reach 3000 tokens and the game stops, but against a fair coin that is luck rather than skill.
