Reading the numbers

  • Crosswind is the part of the wind blowing across the runway, and the side it comes from. It is the same at both ends of the strip.
  • In gusts is the crosswind in the forecast gust, shown only when the gust is stronger than the sustained wind.
  • Headwind on names the end the wind blows down, the one to take off and land into, and how much of the wind is headwind there.
  • Directions are degrees true, as in METARs and TAFs. Runway 17/35 lies at 179°/359° true.
  • The forecast is NOAA's HRRR model in 15-minute steps, six hours from each hourly run, processed at the University of Utah's CHPC. A run reaches this page a few hours after its start time, so steps already in the past are not shown.

Not an official weather briefing. Before flight, get one from Flight Service (1-800-WX-BRIEF) or aviationweather.gov.
